Front matterAbstract

The SAZ Portal is Sanlam Allianz Kenya's web platform for selling and servicing general insurance. It went live on 11 December 2025 as a Motor agency sales tool and now carries two full product lines, Motor and Personal Accident, from quotation through payment, regulatory certification and policy document delivery.

This document records what the system does today, written for a general business reader rather than a technical one. It was compiled directly from the portal's own program code and its engineering records, and from the programme's delivery register, so every claim in it traces back to something that has actually been built. Chapter 1 sets out scope and terms. Chapters 2 to 8 describe the system. Chapter 9 records what has been delivered, and Chapter 10 states current limitations and the planned roadmap. A technical reference appendix follows, for IT and integration teams only.

Chapter 1Introduction

1.1Background

Before the portal existed, Motor insurance was quoted and arranged by hand, with underwriting steps completed manually and the sale keyed directly into Sanlam Allianz's core insurance system, Premia. The portal moves that whole experience online, for agents, bank and broker partners, and members of the public buying directly. In one sitting, it works out the price, takes payment over M-Pesa, issues the official motor certificate required by the industry regulator, notifies the Premia operations team so the policy can be loaded there, and emails the client every document they need.

1.2Purpose and scope

This document describes what the system does today. Anything still being built is described only in Chapter 10, and is always clearly marked as planned rather than delivered. The document covers:

  • How the system is put together, in outline
  • Who uses it, and what each type of user is allowed to do
  • What it sells, and the options available for each product
  • How a sale actually happens, from first quote to delivered policy, for both Motor and Personal Accident, including renewals and direct public sales
  • How clients pay, and how that money is tracked
  • The documents it produces, and the outside organisations it works with to do so

1.3Intended audience

This document is written for a general business reader. Executives and business stakeholders will find everything they need in Chapters 1, 2, 4, 5, 9 and 10. Operations and finance teams will also want Chapters 3, 6 and 7. A final technical reference appendix exists solely for IT and integration specialists; business readers can skip it entirely without losing anything.

Chapter 2System overview

2.1How it is put together

The portal has two parts, built and maintained as one product. The part people see and use — the website and the screens an agent or admin works in — is one piece of software. Behind it sits a second piece of software that does the actual thinking: it holds every business rule, works out prices, talks to M-Pesa and the regulator, and keeps the records. Nothing a client or agent sees in their browser ever holds a password or credential for an outside company; only the engine behind the scenes does that, which keeps those relationships secure.

Both parts are modern, actively maintained web technologies, hosted in the cloud rather than on a single office server, so the portal can be scaled up as the business grows. A dedicated database keeps every quote, policy and transaction, and a separate connection lets the engine look up and list policy data in Premia (P11), the Group's main record-keeping system. Loading a new sale into Premia itself is still handed to the operations team, by an automated email the portal sends at the moment of sale (Chapter 5).

2.2How the pieces connect

Three different kinds of user reach the portal: agents and partners who are logged in, Sanlam Allianz staff and administrators who are logged in, and members of the public who buy directly without an account. Whichever way they arrive, every request passes through the same engine, which is the only part of the system allowed to talk to the outside world. Three regulated integrations — DMVIC, M-Pesa and KRA — are all reached through one shared connection to keep that relationship simple and secure; everything else is connected to directly.

2.4Things the system does on its own

A number of routine tasks run automatically, without anyone needing to trigger them:

  • A daily sales summary is emailed to finance every day.
  • Payments that are left in limbo are checked and resolved every fifteen minutes.
  • Scheduled announcements to agents go out the moment they are due.
  • Old message records are cleared out automatically after thirty days.

3.2How access is controlled

Every specific action in the portal — selling a quote, settling an agent's credit, sending a broadcast, and so on — is its own named permission. A role is simply a bundle of these permissions, and a person's final set of rights is their role's bundle, plus anything granted to them individually, minus anything specifically taken away. A small number of trusted administrators can bypass this entirely. Every change to anyone's access is permanently recorded, so it is always possible to see who changed what, and when. The screens a person cannot use simply do not appear for them, rather than appearing greyed out, and a full, readable list of exactly what each role can do is available inside the portal itself.

3.3Banks and broker partners

A partner organisation, such as a bank, is not treated as a separate kind of user. Its head office is simply an agency administrator in its own right, recognised by its own verified customer code, and each of its branches is an agent linked back to that head office. A branch can itself have sub-branches underneath it, so a partner's own internal structure can be reproduced exactly. Head office and branch accounts are automatically allowed to sell on credit, each against its own credit limit, and an agent carrying unpaid credit cannot be promoted to head office until that is cleared.

3.4Signing in

  1. PasswordThe user enters their password and asks for a one-time code.
  2. One-time codeA code is sent by text message or email, and only a limited number of attempts are allowed.
  3. Signed inOnce the code is confirmed, the portal remembers the session securely so the person is not asked to log in again and again.

Members of the public buying directly never sign in at all. A small, deliberately limited set of actions — getting a quote, looking up a renewal, paying, and receiving documents by email, all covered in Section 5.5 — are the only ones available without an account.

5.3Valuing high-value vehicles

Some vehicles are valuable enough to need an independent valuation before cover is confirmed. At quote time, the client either confirms they already have a recent valuation, or chooses one of our approved valuers: SOLVIT Limited or Links Valuers & Assessors.

  1. Valuer chosenRecorded against the quote.
  2. Cover starts, valuation pendingIf no valuation was already in hand, the policy goes live but is marked as awaiting the valuer's report rather than fully complete.
  3. Valuer carries out the inspectionProgress is tracked automatically as the valuer works through booking, inspection and review, and the result comes back to the portal once the report is ready.
  4. Valuation letter is issuedA formal letter is produced and emailed along with the rest of the documents. Reminders are sent if a client has not yet accepted a completed valuation.

5.4Renewing a Motor policy

  1. List of renewals is loadedStaff bring in the list of policies due for renewal from Premia.
  2. Vehicle is looked upBy number plate. This step is open to the public, so a client can start their own renewal without logging in.
  3. Eligibility is checkedA renewal can be completed up to 14 days after the due date; later than that, it is referred to underwriting instead.
  4. Price is recalculatedAt today's rates, not the old price. Cover that is not fully comprehensive must be paid for in full, not in instalments.
  5. Quote is created from the old policyClient and vehicle details carry over automatically, and the sale still counts toward the original agent or partner.
  6. Payment and issuanceThe same checks, certificate and document pack as a new policy.

5.5Buying directly, without an agent

Members of the public can buy Motor or Personal Accident cover, and renew a Motor policy, entirely on their own, with no account needed. If they arrived through a link or QR code shared by a particular agent or partner, that sale is still credited to them behind the scenes; otherwise it is simply recorded as a direct sale.

  1. Get a quoteThe client fills in the Motor or Personal Accident form on the public website.
  2. PayBy M-Pesa.
  3. Receive the policyDocuments are emailed, and stay available to download again later through a secure link.
What this is, and is notThis is a one-off, guest checkout, not a customer account. There is no login for a member of the public to come back to, and no personal dashboard yet; a full customer portal is planned (Section 10.2).

5.6Buying Personal Accident cover

  1. Quote and identity details capturedThe cover and plan are chosen, and a short form collects identity details, next of kin, and a few health questions.
  2. Price is calculatedFrom the standard Personal Accident rate tables (Table 4.2).
  3. Quote is sentA preview of the cover note and policy schedule is available before payment.
  4. Health checkIf the applicant's answers to the health questions raise a concern, the case is sent to an underwriter instead of being approved automatically.
  5. Client paysUsing the same payment options as Motor.
  6. Tax details verifiedThe applicant's tax identification number is checked automatically, and the application is then either approved or declined.
  7. Documents are emailedThe Personal Accident policy schedule and wording, or a decline notice, as applicable.

If a client later extends an already-issued Personal Accident policy, that extension is approved automatically, since their details were already verified when the policy was first issued.

5.7Extending Motor cover after a valuation

A Motor policy that needed a valuation (Section 5.3) starts out on a one-month certificate at the original sum insured. Once the agent accepts the valuer's figure, the premium is recalculated against the new sum insured, and the client owes the difference before the policy can carry a full annual certificate. Paying that difference is what "extending cover" means here — it is not a separate product, and it does not touch SAZ Assist or the Premia notification, both of which already happened once at first issue.

  1. Extend-cover screen opensOnly available once the valuation has been accepted; the amount due is always fetched fresh from the server, never calculated in the browser.
  2. Client pays the differenceBy M-Pesa or on-account, flagged as an extension rather than a first payment.
  3. Certificate is reissued for the full termThe same DMVIC check-and-issue call as new business runs again, now covering the full annual period instead of one month.
  4. Updated documents are emailedA fresh policy schedule, at the new premium and sum insured.

The amount a client must pay is always worked out by the system itself, so it cannot be short-paid, and part-payments are not accepted for a single instalment. Whichever way the payment is made, it triggers the same sequence afterwards: the sale is finalised, the certificate is issued, any valuation is kicked off, the client is enrolled in SAZ Assist, and the documents are sent.

6.3Managing agency credit

Finance clears an agency's outstanding credit either one sale at a time, or in bulk against a single payment, and this is purely a bookkeeping step; it never changes a policy that has already been issued. Outstanding credit is tracked by age — current, over 30 days, over 60 days, and over 90 days overdue — so finance can see exactly where the risk sits. An agent can see their own balance, a partner head office can see all of its branches, and finance can export the full picture for reporting.

7.2How documents and messages reach people

  • Email carries the full document pack, quote links, and any referrals or reminders.
  • Text message carries login codes, SAZ Assist activation confirmations, and broadcast announcements.
  • Secure links let a client reopen their documents in a browser at any time, without needing to log in.
  • In-app alerts keep portal users informed, for example the moment a certificate is issued.

Every email and text message sent is logged, so there is a record of who was sent what, when, and whether it was delivered successfully. That record is kept for 30 days; the content of the message itself is not stored.

7.3Proactive notices and broadcasts

The system sends reminders for expiring DMVIC certificates and for valuations awaiting acceptance. Administrators send scheduled email or SMS broadcasts, with attachments, to selected agent and client pools.

Chapter 8Integrations: what the portal connects to

The portal depends on a number of integrations to do its job properly. Some are run and configured entirely by our own ICT team; others belong to outside organisations. Of those outside connections, some are reached directly, and some are reached through a middleware server — a piece of connecting software our ICT team builds and manages — rather than the portal talking to them one-to-one. The full technical detail of how each connection works is kept in the appendix, for IT reference only; what matters for a business reader is set out below.

8.5Keeping these connections secure

Credentials for every integration are kept in one secure, central place, never scattered through the system's code, and are refreshed automatically before they expire so a connection never silently stops working. Every single call made to an integration, whether direct, through the middleware, or through the Flip gateway, is logged for audit purposes, with any sensitive details hidden, so there is always a full record of what was asked and when.

A.6Credential and token management

Tokens are held in one database table (ExternalApiToken, one row per provider), not in code. Each server keeps an in-memory copy and refreshes it five minutes before expiry; the middleware token is renewed through the two-step login in Table A.2. Every external call is logged with credentials redacted, giving a complete audit trail. AURA and Flip use staging or demo hosts in development configuration; production hosts are set per environment.
